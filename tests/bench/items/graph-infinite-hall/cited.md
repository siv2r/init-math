- Hall's theorem for finitely many sets (P. Hall 1935; the inductive proof of Halmos and Vaughan, "The marriage problem", Amer. J. Math. 72 (1950), does not use finiteness of the sets): a finite family of sets A_1, ..., A_n, each of arbitrary cardinality, has a system of distinct representatives if and only if |A_(i_1) union ... union A_(i_k)| >= k for every choice of distinct indices i_1, ..., i_k.
- König's infinity lemma (Diestel, Graph Theory, 5th ed., Lemma 8.1.2): let V_0, V_1, ... be an infinite sequence of disjoint non-empty finite sets, and let G be a graph on their union. Assume that every vertex v in a set V_n with n >= 1 has a neighbour f(v) in V_(n-1). Then G contains a ray v_0 v_1 ... with v_n in V_n for all n.
